Case architecture

Compared with the previous version (530×440×464), the case is 60 mm deeper and 16 mm taller. The lid is now a narrower central assembly between fixed shoulders. This clears the door pockets and maintains continuous side walls, including when the lid is stowed. The lid proportions were deliberately revised. Fine joint lines remain visible; zero clearance cannot be guaranteed in wood.

The front retains three levels: two doors for the SwissKubik compartment, an independent drawer, and the lid with its front lip and fixed shoulders. The lip closes 2 mm above the drawer front. When stowed, the lip sits at the rear, with its top at 480 mm, flush with the shoulders. A fixed rail above the drawer closes the front opening left by the raised lip.

The igus requirements for operating force and its distance from the guide (the 2:1 rule), mounting orientation, combined load, and wear still need application calculations. Applying force at an unsuitable grip position can bind a plain-bearing guide even when its envelope fits. The damper does not support the lid or replace a lock or counterbalance. ACE requires appropriate shaft support rather than unsupported radial or axial loading of the damper shaft.

Construction, materials, and finish

The exact pre-maple top-and-lid parquet appearance is the finish reference for all visible wooden surfaces, including the case, lip, doors, drawer front, glass surrounds, back, and visible interior timber. The original procedural pattern, colors, 48 mm scale, grain, and cell arrangement have been restored verbatim. Matching that appearance takes priority over maple as a species. Select the veneer species and hardwood frame stock against a physical sample; the rendered pattern does not identify a real wood species. Wide panels retain quality birch plywood cores with balanced veneers. Preserve the existing finished thicknesses, allowing for veneer and adhesive. Concealed mortise-and-tenon frame joints, captured panels, half-blind drawer dovetails, and mechanism clearances are unchanged.

Low-iron tempered glass, 4 mm thick, with polished edges, glazing pads, and removable retaining strips. No direct glass-to-metal contact. Glazing grooves are included in the geometry. Retaining strips, corner connections, and screw recesses still need workshop drawings. Temper only after measuring completed frames. Apply a thin satin finish, acclimatize the parts, and make final adjustments after finishing. Quiet seals reduce dust ingress; a hermetic seal is not promised.

Viewing and source code

The standalone HTML works offline. It provides seven motion sliders, mechanism isolation, transparent case walls, an exploded assembly, joinery studies, 1×/2×/3× rendering, full-screen mode, and PNG snapshots at 3840 pixels wide. Broad fill lighting and roughness-dependent highlights are included. The wood texture is procedural; color and gloss are not approved physical material samples.

The standard GLB contains separate parts, PBR glass/wood/metal materials, and seven animation clips. Each clip starts closed and includes prerequisite opening motions. Play clips separately. It supports further visualization and is not parametric CAD or manufacturing documentation. An embedded 2048×2048 parquet albedo texture is baked by the same original GLSL used by the HTML. Its finite atlas covers the cabinet coordinates without folding the original cell indices into a repeating 20-cell pattern. Per-face UVs retain the existing orientation and scale. Both versions remain visualizations pending a physical material sample.

The source is split into dimensions and motion sequencing, geometry, transforms, display materials and interface, exports, and checks. Edit the modular source and rebuild the HTML rather than maintaining a large hand-edited combined file. The HTML interface, embedded specification, material descriptions, accessibility labels, and CSV export labels are in English.

Three.js is a possible next step for environment lighting and shadows in the browser. Blender Cycles can produce photorealistic product images from the GLB. Neither verifies hardware fit. Manufacturing confidence requires CAD with manufacturer parts, installation drawings, and a prototype. Nothing needs to be installed to use the supplied HTML.

Materials and cost

The CSV lists installed quantities, spare stock, and purchase quantities. Known prices from the previous edition remain labeled as estimates; no new supplier quotations were obtained. The additional veneer allowance is included. New lid components remain unpriced until part numbers and mounting details are selected. The restored reference finish is a visual/material revision only: dimensions, clearances, and mechanism motion have not changed. The lid mass, counterbalance force, and hinge torque require checking against the actual timber construction. Veneer and solid frame stock require fresh quotations after the species and grade have been selected to match the reference, and are excluded from the known subtotal. Known priced rows total ILS 3,891–6,025, including spare stock. This is not the complete material cost. Labor, design, prototyping, shipping, taxes, SwissKubik units, and watches are excluded.
